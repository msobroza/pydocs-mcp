3. #394 — the $0 zero-hit count

The floor

A search zero hit can cost the agent at most one Turn: the follow-up call the Pointer invites. So an arm that changes the Pointer can move the mean by at most (share of tasks with a zero hit) × 1 Turn. Two identical arms already differ by about ±0.6 Turns at 20 tasks. The floor is the share at which the largest possible effect reaches that noise edge: 12 of 20 tasks on repoqa-qa/small_dev.

For a future 100-task repoqa-qa/dev arm the same rule gives about 27 of 100. No such arm exists in the stored runs, so nothing was scored against it.

What was counted

  • Search zero hit: a search_codebase call that did not fail and returned no rows, read from the call record in the trace (hit_count == 0, empty result_ids). The fired-rule log was not the source.
  • Zero-hit task: a task with at least one search zero hit. A grep or glob with no match does not count.
  • Slice: the four complete repoqa-qa/small_dev ladder arms copied from Max's machine, 20 tasks each, chat model qwen/qwen3.8-27b.

Label for every primary figure below: the four ladder arms were recorded before ADR 0027 (the API zero-hit fallback, merged 2026-11-08) and before system_v3 (2026-11-07). They are the only complete small_dev ladder traces available and the right slice for the 12-of-20 floor. They bound today's rate from above.

What the rows show

  • Every search zero hit is an API zero hit. All 22 are kind="api" requests, each the task's opening search in Turn 1. None of the 63 searches of any other kind came back empty.
  • The count itself is noisy. A1 and A2 are the same commit with the same settings and show 5 and 9.
  • The Pointer is rarely followed. get_overview was the next call in 2 of 22 zero-hit tasks — the only two where the miss was sent alone in its Turn. In the other 20 the model had sent a grep or a second search alongside and carried on from that result.
  • The Needle was still reached. The gold file reached the model in all 22 tasks, and 19 of 22 answers cite the Needle. The three misses are all on the two generated-code mlc-llm needles, which no arm cites, zero hit or not.
  • Zero-hit tasks take more Turns, and that is a different problem. The gap (for example 5.33 against 2.55 Turns in A2) belongs to opening with a descriptive kind="api" query. The arms of #394 would have changed one line of the response. ADR 0027 already answers the API zero hit itself.

7. #391 — the BACKBONE rewrite and the other heads

What is rewritten

The seed keeps two passages in BACKBONE that tell the agent to search more broadly (python/pydocs_mcp/harness/core/skills/search_guidance_seed.md, lines 25–34):

  • "Decompose before retrieving: at most three sharp search queries … two focused queries beat one sprawling sentence that mixes concerns."
  • "Spend a ~5-document precision budget: keep about five retrieved documents in play at a time …"

The direction named in the grill: decompose only when the question holds several behaviours, and state the five-document budget as a ceiling. The reason: this is the kind of breadth advice that raised a Qwen model's tool calls by up to 44% in the literature the grill read (2611.22539), while the baseline already shows the Needle in a turn-1 result in 27 of 29 repoqa tasks.

This changes an earlier decision. The decision log (B2) left BACKBONE policy to the multitask optimizer and limited hand edits to the "already shown" definition. Question 4, option 3 overrides that for the breadth sentences. Two rules stay binding: BACKBONE must not grow (543 of 1,000 tokens today), and each edit replaces a whole section with a per-section token ledger for all ten sections.

Who reads BACKBONE

Guidance folds in a fixed order: BACKBONE, then the task head, then the harness-and-task head (python/pydocs_mcp/harness/core/guidance_fold.py). Every harness and task therefore reads the rewritten sentences. This experiment measures one of the six.

8. #391 — the acceptance rule

The current rule, as coded

The compare verb returns PASS when all of these hold (benchmarks/src/pydocs_eval/campaign/before_after_acceptance.py):

  • penalised Turns-to-answer goes down — a point estimate, of any size;
  • the budget-exhausted rate does not rise;
  • needle cited stays within the band below baseline: 0.050, one task of 20.

The three clauses added for this experiment

ClauseWhy
(a)A PASS whose tokens-in per task is above baseline flips the default only with Max's recorded sign-off.The knob adds tokens to every Turn. Its break-even is a Turn reduction of about 12–15%, roughly 0.4–0.6 Turns per task. Below that, each question costs more while the rule still passes.
(b)No unanswered outcome class may rise: token-limit stops (starved_reply) and timeouts, as well as budget exhaustion.Today only turn-limit stops are guarded. A token-limit or timeout stop counts 13 in the mean, about +0.45 at 20 tasks, and can be outweighed by gains elsewhere. Long prompts are where token-limit stops appear.
(c)A PASS whose Turn gain is under 0.6 is re-run on the 100-task repoqa-qa/dev slice before the flip.Two identical arms moved 0.35 Turns apart, and a change under about ±0.6 is inside the noise at 20 tasks. A variant with no effect passes the Turns clause about half the time.

One model stays. No second-model arm is added. The winning PR's changelog states that the evidence is one model on one slice and that guidance_task: none restores the old prompt.
